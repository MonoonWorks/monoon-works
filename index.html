Academia Flow — from a reading list to a research project

A local-first desktop workspace for the work that happens between finding a source and finishing a manuscript. Flow keeps reading, source-linked notes, chapter writing, and project planning close enough to remain part of the same research process.

For a future version, we’re planning optional Claude API support to compare claims across selected research notes and review draft paragraphs against their source material, with the researcher choosing what to send and what to keep.

01

1. Find the reading that matters now

A long project quickly outgrows a flat bibliography. In Flow, books and papers can be searched by title or author, narrowed by tags, and separated into collected, to-read, reading, and finished states. This makes the library clearly show the current research workload instead of only recording what has been saved.

Opening a reading record brings its bibliographic details, local PDF, publisher link, and citation file into one view. The researcher can check the source before reading and keep the file needed for the next step attached to the same record.

02

2. Keep an interpretation tied to its source

The difficult part of reading is returning to the exact passage behind an idea. Flow keeps the PDF and research notes in the same reading workspace. Quotations can carry page references, so a later claim can be checked against the source instead of relying on memory or an isolated sentence copied elsewhere.

Notes can use headings, tables, links, footnotes, equations, and diagrams when the argument needs structure. If the reading came from Academia Archive, Flow can reopen the linked source record to recover its wider archival or bibliographic context. The software preserves the connection; the researcher still develops the interpretation.

03

3. Develop notes into chapter writing

Research notes become useful when they can be tested inside a larger argument. Flow includes a manuscript workspace for drafting by chapter, with support for headings, footnotes, citations, and figures. The aim is to move from source work into sustained writing without losing the references that made the draft possible.

For researchers who write in Microsoft Word, the companion add-in supports source search, citations, footnotes, endnotes, and bibliographies. It carries source work into a familiar writing environment while the researcher decides how evidence is arranged and what belongs in the manuscript.

04

4. Make the next research session visible

A dissertation or book project unfolds over months, so knowing the next action matters as much as keeping a long plan. Flow lets the researcher set the day’s Big 3 and a practical first action, then place reading, writing, meetings, and other research tasks into a weekly plan.

Daily records preserve what happened when the project is interrupted and resumed. Returning to the workspace means seeing both the larger project and the work left from the previous session. The researcher can decide, record, and revisit the rhythm of the work in one place.

Academia Archive — preserve the source and its context

Archive manages photographs, scans, PDFs, books, articles, and archival records with fields suited to each source type. Authors and contributor roles, dates, publication details, identifiers, and attachments stay with the record. Transcriptions, quotations, and research notes can be attached to a source, file, or image region, then found with metadata, tags, and local OCR text.

Collections and related-record links preserve context across a growing library. Archive can import Tropy exports and Zotero or Juris-M records, connect selected sources to Flow, and support citation work through the Word connection.

Simple PDF — read the text without losing the endnotes

Simple PDF is a macOS reader built for the back-and-forth of scholarly books. The main text remains at the current page while a separate endnotes pane opens elsewhere in the document, so checking a note does not repeatedly displace the argument being read.

The two panes can show different pages, and the outline helps move between chapters. The screenshot below uses a synthetic document, with page 1 held beside the notes on page 10.

We’re also planning optional Claude API support for translating selected passages or pages, summarizing a paper’s question and argument, and asking follow-up questions while keeping the original text and page references close at hand.

Simple Note — move between quick capture and structured notes

Simple Note is a local note editor with rich-text and Markdown source modes. Headings, checklists, tables, quotations, and code blocks support notes that need more structure, while autosave keeps short thoughts from becoming a separate filing task.

Notes can be pinned, tagged, sorted, and searched by title or body. Local Markdown and text files can be opened or imported, and notes can be exported as Markdown for use elsewhere. Focus mode, duplication, trash, and restore cover everyday note maintenance.

Simple Search — find Korean file names from the menu bar

Simple Search opens from the macOS menu bar or a global shortcut. It supports Korean initial-consonant, partial-name, and in-progress composition searches, then narrows results with file-type filters for formats such as PDF, PowerPoint, Word, Excel, and HWP.

The index uses file names and metadata rather than reading document contents or downloading cloud-file bodies. Keyboard navigation leads to open, Show in Finder, Quick Look, and copy-path actions. Saved searches, recent searches, and folder-scoped search make recurring retrieval tasks easier to repeat.

Built from graduate-student research

HVN · Founder & Developer

HVN is a graduate student, researcher, and independent developer. Monoon Works began with a practical problem from that work: a bibliography alone does not produce an argument. Researchers must read the source, preserve page references, develop interpretations in notes, and connect those notes to chapters over months of work.

Zotero, Tropy, and EndNote each remain useful. The question behind these products is how bibliographic records, source material, notes, writing, and planning can stay connected as a humanities or social science project grows. Development began in July 2025.

Questions & answers

How do Flow and Archive work together?

Archive organizes source records, files, and their context. Flow brings reading, research notes, chapter writing, and planning into one workspace. Selected Archive records can be linked to Flow.

Can I keep using Zotero and Microsoft Word?

Yes. Archive can import Zotero records, and a separate Word add-in supports literature search, citations, footnotes, and bibliographies. These tools can remain part of your workflow.

Where is my research stored?

Flow stores its research workspace locally on your device. Archive also keeps its source files and database locally. Optional connections, such as Zotero sync, are configured separately.

Can I change the appearance?

Yes. Academia Flow offers multiple light, dark, and special themes, with Light, Dark, and Auto modes. The Flow screenshots on this page use Academic Neutral.

How can I try it?

The products are currently used in a small private pilot. Email contact@monoonworks.com with the product you are interested in and a little about your research workflow to ask about availability.
